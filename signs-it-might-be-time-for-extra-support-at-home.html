5 Signs It Might Be Time for a Little Extra Support at Home

← Back to all articles
18 August 2026  ·  4 min read

Deciding whether an older parent or family member needs a bit more help at home can be difficult. Often there’s no single dramatic moment — just a series of small changes that add up. Noticing them early means you can put gentle support in place before a small problem becomes a bigger one.

Here are five signs that a little extra help around the home might make life easier and safer.

1. The house isn’t being kept the way it used to be

If dishes are piling up, laundry is going undone, or the home feels less tidy than usual, it can be a sign that everyday tasks are becoming harder to manage. This isn’t about judgement — keeping on top of a house takes energy, and a hand with cleaning and laundry can lift a real weight.

2. Meals are being skipped or simplified

An empty fridge, out-of-date food, or a shift to toast and tea instead of proper meals can suggest that shopping and cooking have become a struggle. Support with meal preparation and groceries helps make sure they’re still eating well.

3. Appointments and outings are being missed

If someone has stopped driving or finds public transport tiring, they may quietly start skipping medical appointments, shopping trips or social catch-ups. Reliable transport can keep them connected to the things and people that matter.

4. Personal care is becoming harder

Tasks like showering, dressing or grooming can become tiring or unsafe over time. If you notice changes here, respectful personal care support can help someone stay comfortable and keep their dignity.

5. They seem more isolated or low

Spending more time alone, losing interest in hobbies, or seeming flat can be signs of loneliness. Regular friendly company — even just a chat over a cuppa — can make a genuine difference to someone’s wellbeing.

Starting the conversation

If a few of these feel familiar, it may be worth a gentle chat about what support could look like. It doesn’t have to mean a big change — often a couple of hours a week is enough to make daily life easier and give the whole family peace of mind.

If you’d like to talk it through, we’re always happy to have a friendly, no-obligation conversation about what might help.

This article shares general information to help families and is not a substitute for personalised professional or medical advice. If you have concerns about someone’s health or safety, please speak with their GP or a qualified professional.
